## Cash reserve

**Status: approved by me, Nov. 3, 2026: a fixed 15% with no market gauge, the change to `sizing`, and the 24-month review.** Changing the method needs my approval. The figures are a judgment, not a measured result. A lesson, not advice, and not a market forecast. Page: `cash-reserve.html`.

**The method: keep 15% of the portfolio in cash as normal and never spend the last 5% on a buy. The market's level plays no part.**

### Cash as a position

- **What it is for:** to pay for the next hit, and to make saying no cheap to live with (rule 4).
- **What it costs:** what it could have earned elsewhere. $100,000 from Oct. 2006 to Oct. 2026 became $138,813 in Treasury bills and $816,987 in the S&P 500 with dividends, and $166,391 was needed to keep up with prices (calc., before tax, [Shiller, file dated Oct. 6, 2026, fetched Nov. 3, 2026](https://shillerdata.com/); [Treasury daily bill rates, through Nov. 2, 2026, fetched Nov. 3, 2026](https://home.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/interest-rates/TextView?type=daily_treasury_bill_rates&field_tdr_date_value=2026)).
- **What would prove it wrong:** years with no hit while cash stays high. See the review below.
- It does not count toward the 5 to 10 positions in rule 2.
- Cash means Treasury bills or the like. It is not a place to reach for yield.

### The levels

Cash as a share of the portfolio, stocks at today's prices.

| Level | Share | What may spend it | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Above normal | Over 15% | Any hit that clears the gates | Cash that has no other job |
| Normal | 5% to 15% | A Strong thesis only | Up to one Middling position at its 10% cap |
| Floor | Under 5% | Nothing | It equals the Thin cap, one small position |

### When a buy-below is hit

1. **Check it is a real hit.** Refresh value from the newest 10-Q or 10-K, then run the three gates in `sizing`.
2. **Size it** by `sizing`. That is the most it can be. No schedule and no averaging in.
3. **Pay from cash above 15%.** Any grade.
4. **From 15% down to 5%: Strong only.** Thin and Middling stop at 15% and buy smaller, or not at all.
5. **Never spend the floor on a buy.**
6. **Several hits at once:** highest score first, then highest coverage.

Never borrow to pay for a buy, and never sell a sound position to pay for one.

### Refilling

Only from new money, interest, dividends, trims or the sale of a broken thesis. Under 15% is not a breach. It only limits the next buy. There is no deadline to refill.

### Alerts

- **Cash under the floor,** at a refresh: tell me and add nothing. It never forces a sale.
- **Cash above 50% for 24 months in a row:** tell me and ask whether my search is wide enough. It never loosens the buy test. The bigger cost is cash with no ceiling, not the reserve. At a 50% loss if wrong a position is 10%, so five positions leave 50% in cash (calc.).

### What the evidence does and does not say

- **Waiting has a price.** Over the twenty years, bills beat the index in 42 of 229 one-year stretches, 8 of 181 five-year stretches and 0 of 121 ten-year stretches, all overlapping (calc., same sources).
- **Waiting sometimes wins.** From Jan. 2000 to Dec. 2009, bills made +31.8% and the S&P 500 −6.9% (calc., same sources, and [Damodaran, Jan. 2026, fetched Nov. 3, 2026](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datafile/histretSP.html)).
- **Why no gauge.** Moving the reserve on the Shiller CAPE did not earn its place. Against a fixed 15%, reserves of 5%, 15% and 25% ended $9,810 behind from Oct. 2006 and $24,714 ahead from Jan. 2000 (calc., same sources, an index sum that sells to reset, which my rules bar). Over one year it sorted little: starting at 30 or more, bills won 34% of one-year stretches, 53% of five-year stretches and 61% of ten-year stretches. Starting under 20: 30%, 13% and 6% (calc., same sources). The ten-year row at 30 or more is close to two events, 1929 and 1997 to 2002. So no exception to override 5 is needed, and rule 6 stands untouched.

### What it does not do

- It makes no call on where the market goes.
- It makes no buy case. Only `when-to-buy` does, and `sizing` sets the size.
- It sets no stock-bond mix and no target for stocks. Cash above 15% stays cash until a buy clears.
- It never makes a sale. Selling stays with `when-to-trim` and a broken thesis (override 7).
