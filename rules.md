# My Investing Rules

1. **Hold for five years or more.** A business needs years to prove a thesis, and the price can ignore it until then.
2. **Keep 5 to 10 positions, up to 20 when there is good reason.** That is few enough to know each one well and enough that one mistake cannot sink the portfolio.
3. **Long only: no short selling.** A short has capped gains and open-ended losses, and it costs money to hold for years while a good business can compound.
4. **Always keep a cash reserve, so fear of missing out never forces a buy.** With cash on hand I can always buy later, so I never have to rush in now.
5. **Size each position by how strong the thesis is and how much I lose if it is wrong.** A strong thesis earns a bigger position, but the possible loss sets how big I can afford to go.
6. **Act on the thesis, not the price.** The price shows what others will pay today, while the thesis shows whether my reason for owning it still holds.
