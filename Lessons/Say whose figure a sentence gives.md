# Say whose figure a sentence gives

*Written 2026-11-03.*

## What happened

The strategic researcher caught this, and Claude confirmed it. [[Corning]] said Amphenol says CommScope secured supply "and raised its 2026 forecast to $5.1B". That reads as Amphenol's forecast. The $5.1B is CommScope's business alone, and Amphenol's own guidance is $36.9B to $37.1B ([Amphenol Q3 release, Oct. 28, 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000820313/000110465926119421/aph-ex99d1.htm)). No decision used it. The fundamental researcher also found that the note's $2,701M of contract liabilities is a sum of two lines and was not marked calc. The filing's text says "$2.7 billion" ([10-Q, filed Oct. 29, 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/24741/000002474126000279/glw-20260930.htm)).

## What it taught us

When one company's figure sits beside another's in a sentence, name the company it belongs to. A sum is calc. even when the parts are filed.

Both are fixed in [[Corning]], under "Who loses if Corning wins" and "Catalysts and contract footprints". [[Amphenol]] and `rivals.html` already say CommScope, so they stay as written. No rule changed.
