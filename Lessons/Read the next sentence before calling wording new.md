# Read the next sentence before calling wording new

*Written 2026-11-03.*

## What happened

Claude caught this at the merge. The fundamental researcher's report said Corning "now" expects to repatriate foreign earnings, and it quoted July's "We do not foresee a need to repatriate" as the baseline. The next sentence in the [10-Q, filed July 29, 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/24741/000002474126000255/glw-20260630.htm), says Corning does repatriate from subsidiaries whose earnings are not permanently reinvested. The change in the [10-Q, filed Oct. 29, 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/24741/000002474126000279/glw-20260930.htm), is narrower: some earnings are "no longer permanently reinvested". It reached the proposal stage only in corrected form, so no note carried the wrong version.

## What it taught us

When two filings are compared, read the whole passage in both. A quote that shows the change clearly may leave out the sentence that shrinks it. A second reading against the filing is worth doing on a report's lead claim.
