# Take out both sides of a deposit

*Written 2026-11-03.*

## What happened

Claude caught this while testing the claims in [[Corning]] for `edge.html`. The note says nine-month free cash flow without the deposit was "little changed": about $841M against $793M. The sums are right, but they remove only the $1.0 billion that came in during 2026. Repayments of older deposits stay in the figure. The filed line "Customer deposits and government incentives" was +$639M against −$49M ([10-Q, filed Oct. 29, 2026][q]). Without that whole line, the figures are $1,202M against $842M, up 43% (calc.). That line also holds government incentives, and the filing does not split them, so neither figure is clean. No decision used the "little changed" reading.

## What it taught us

When taking a flow out of cash, look at the filed line as well as the inflow, or the result can show a slowdown that the deduction itself created. Show both readings until a filing separates them.

## What changed

Nothing in [[Corning]] at first, because I asked that the note not change in that session. On Nov. 3, 2026 I asked for the correction. [[Corning]] now shows both readings in the summary's words, "Figures to watch", the cash flow section and the balance sheet section. The original table in "Figures to watch" stays, because I chose that measure, and the second reading sits under it. Later that day `cash.html`, `edge.html` and the dashboard's panel were updated too, and I added a third reading, labelled an estimate. No rule changed.
